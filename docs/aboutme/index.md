#About Me

I am Alinah Sarratt, and I am currently a junior at the University of North Carolina at Charlotte studying mechanical engineering with a concentration in biomedical engineering. I am also obtaining a minor in chemistry alongside my major. As an engineer, I would describe myself as analytical, creative, and driven, especially when it comes to finding new ways to innovate products that can aid individuals in the healthcare field. While I have developed a general mechanical engineering background through my coursework, my main area of interest is now biomedical engineering and how mechanical engineering principles can be applied to medicine.

I joined engineering mostly because I was interested in the research and development side of the field. The area that stood out to me the most was the creation and optimization of medical devices. I originally started college studying chemical engineering but later switched to mechanical engineering because I wanted to gain more knowledge about biomechanics, orthopedics, and the mechanical properties that go into designing medical devices. Making this switch allowed me to focus more on how engineering can directly relate to the human body and improve patient care.

I became interested in this field because I believe there are still many gaps in the medical field, including devices used to test for diseases, technology that can assist with medical procedures, and research focused on symptom relief and treatments for different illnesses. In the future, my goal is to work for an R&D company or conduct clinical engineering research. In either area, I hope to contribute to the improvement and development of medical technology. I am especially interested in working on projects related to biomechanics, including joint replacements, orthopedic implants, tissue engineering, and other devices that can improve a patient's function and overall quality of life.
